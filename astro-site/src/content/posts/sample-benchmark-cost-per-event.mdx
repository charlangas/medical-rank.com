## What this sample shows

Four invented accounts, each with a month of Google Ads spend and the Conversions total. The point is the layout: a table, a methodology block, and a short reading guide. It is not a finding about any real practice.

## The figures (HYPOTHETICAL data)

| Invented account | Spend | Conversions column | Spend per event | What the events are |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $2,400 | 24 | $100 | Form submits and calls of 30 seconds or more |
| 2 | $3,800 | 40 | $95 | Forms, two call actions, and one custom goal |
| 3 | $1,500 | 50 | $30 | Includes taps on a phone number |
| 4 | $4,200 | 21 | $200 | Calls of 60 seconds or more only |

## How to read it

Spend per event is arithmetic on a platform count. In this invented example, Account 3's low figure would come from counting taps, and Google says a tap on a number counts the click, not a call. Account 4's high figure would come from counting only longer calls. Spend that is the same can produce different event counts depending on what each account counts.

<EvidenceNote source="call-conv" />

So the column can't be ranked, and none of it is spend per booking. Compare an account with its own earlier months only, and only if its event definitions didn't change.
